Ethics

Research ethics is the foundation of all research. While most of the responsibility is with our PIs and customers to handle the necessary ethics consideration, we are expected to know enough to support them.

The definition of “engineer” includes combining scientific and practical knowledge to produce a useful, efficient, and socially useful outcome. Just like every engineer should be at least a bit aware of basic practices, even outside their direct responsibilities [for example: to be aware if corners are being cut during construction], us Research Engineers need to ensure that our “buildings don’t collapse” (meaning, there are negative outcomes for research or society). Equally, we should apply the engineering mindset to get that outcome efficiently - not overbuilding, but helping to adapt the project to the regulations to create the best overall solution.

Software and data is the expression of the research methods, and thus we are often called upon to help develop, define, and describe the methods for our customers (not just do what they request). Everyone needs to know the basics, and our team must have a few experts in these topics.

Basic expectations

  1. Have a basic knowledge of responsible research, including ethics, integrity, security, and legal matters. This is by reading the following things:

  2. Keep an eye out for projects which may have responsible research considerations, and provide the customer general information about where to look next.

  3. Ensure that projects are build responsibly. Call in an expert when needed, especially during the early design phase.

  4. Learn more and become the expert for cases that suit your interests and projects.

  5. Inform a higher authority (supervisor, research integrity advisors, etc.) for problems which can not be solved through normal channels.

Basic required reading

These three documents are foundational and we expect everyone to read and apply them (but not memorize them).

  • ALLEA code of conduct for research integriety (PDF) - basic expectations within research.

  • EU ethics self-assessment [long document, what to read: you do not need to read everything. Focus on the first part of each section that explains the broad consideration and the checkbox tables. Read more than that when it is interesting.]. This is only for EU funding, but the checklist is a good overview of what to look for.

  • The twelve questions on the Research support triage checklist. This is a good guide when supporting projects.
