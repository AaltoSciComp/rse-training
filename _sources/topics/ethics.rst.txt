Ethics
======

Research ethics is the foundation of all research.  While most of the
responsibility is with our PIs and customers to handle the necessary
ethics consideration, we are expected to know enough to support them.

The definition of "engineer" includes combining scientific and
practical knowledge to produce a useful, efficient, and socially
useful outcome.  Just like every engineer should be at least a bit
aware of basic practices, even outside their direct responsibilities
:inote:`[for example] to be aware if corners are being cut during
construction`, us Research Engineers need to ensure that our
"buildings don't collapse" (meaning, there are negative outcomes for
research or society).  Equally, we should apply the engineering
mindset to get that outcome efficiently - not overbuilding, but
helping to adapt the project to the regulations to create the best
overall solution.

Software and data is the expression of the research methods, and thus
we are often called upon to help develop, define, and describe the
methods for our customers (not just do what they request).  Everyone
needs to know the basics, and our team must have a few experts in
these topics.



Basic expectations
------------------

1. Have a basic knowledge of responsible research, including ethics,
   integrity, security, and legal matters.  This is by reading the
   following things:

2. Keep an eye out for projects which may have responsible research
   considerations, and provide the customer general information about
   where to look next.

3. Ensure that projects are build responsibly.  Call in an expert when
   needed, especially during the early design phase.

4. Learn more and become the expert for cases that suit your interests
   and projects.

5. Inform a higher authority (supervisor, research integrity advisors,
   etc.) for problems which can not be solved through normal channels.


Basic required reading
----------------------

These three documents are foundational and we expect everyone to read
and apply them (but not memorize them).

* `ALLEA code of conduct for research integriety
  <https://allea.org/code-of-conduct/>`__ (`PDF
  <https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf>`__) -
  basic expectations within research.
* `EU ethics self-assessment
  <https://ec.europa.eu/info/funding-tenders/opportunities/docs/2021-2027/common/guidance/how-to-complete-your-ethics-self-assessment_en.pdf>`__
  :inote:`[long document, what to read] you do not need to read
  everything.  Focus on the first part of each section that explains
  the broad consideration and the checkbox tables.  Read more than
  that when it is interesting.`.  This is only for EU funding, but the
  checklist is a good overview of what to look for.
* The twelve questions on the `Research support triage checklist
  <https://eglerean.github.io/ethicscourse/#:~:text=Research%20support%20triage%20checklist>`__.
  This is a good guide when supporting projects.
